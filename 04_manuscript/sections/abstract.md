# Abstract

**Background:** The rapid proliferation of large language models (LLMs) in healthcare education since ChatGPT's release in November 2022 has created an urgent need for systematic landscape mapping. No bibliometric analysis has examined the intersection of LLMs, healthcare simulation, and non-technical skills (NTS) training. This study addresses that gap using exclusively free, open-access data sources.

**Methods:** Seven databases (OpenAlex, PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, CORE, DOAJ) were searched using 35 query strategies. Two-stage screening produced a three-level corpus: extended (7,893 papers), primary (3,587 with simulation terms), and core (3,000 high/medium confidence, Tier 2/3 only). Analyses included publication trends, Lotka's and Bradford's laws, Callon's strategic diagram, citation analysis, geographic distribution, and NTS classification. The pipeline is publicly available on GitHub.

**Results:** From 100,277 records, 86,635 unique papers were identified after deduplication (13.6%). The core corpus demonstrated 38.0% compound annual growth, with 93.8% of output post-ChatGPT, peaking at 1,366 papers in 2025. The corpus achieved an h-index of 91 with 45,900 citations across 12,488 authors. Author productivity followed Lotka's law (beta=2.602, R-squared=0.955); journal distribution conformed to Bradford's law across 1,299 journals. Decision-making and communication dominated NTS research, while crisis resource management (4 papers) and situational awareness were critically under-researched. The urology sub-analysis found 102 papers but zero combining LLMs with simulation boot camps.

**Conclusions:** This analysis reveals a rapidly expanding but unevenly distributed research landscape characterised by proprietary model dominance (42.7% vs 2.8% open-source), geographic concentration in the United States and China, and significant gaps in NTS domains critical to patient safety. Only 30 randomised controlled trials (1.0%) were identified, underscoring the early-stage nature of the evidence base. The replicable, open-access pipeline democratises bibliometric analysis for independent researchers and enables ongoing landscape monitoring as this field matures.

**Keywords:** bibliometric analysis; large language models; healthcare simulation; non-technical skills; medical education; open access
